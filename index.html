Guide 1 · Briefing

How to brief a UGC creator for a product video

A creator video is won or lost in the brief — and the brief that works is one page, not a script. Its job is to hand over everything the creator needs to be accurate, then leave them room to sound like themselves. Here is the structure this desk uses on every store batch:

What goes in the brief

  • The product and its buyer, one line each. "A leave-in conditioner for curly hair, for women who stopped straightening" directs a video; three paragraphs of brand history direct nothing.
  • One problem per video. Pick the single angle this video exists to land. A video about everything converts on nothing.
  • Three must-show moments. The texture, the product in real use, the result next to something familiar for scale. Creators shoot better when they know which shots are non-negotiable.
  • The never-say list. Claims you cannot back up, promises your category restricts, competitor names. This line protects you, not just the creator.
  • Format basics. Vertical 9:16, the product in use in the opening moment, captions on — a large share of viewing happens on mute.

What ships with it

The product itself, early — filming cannot start before it arrives. If a post or ad of yours already performed, send it as a tone reference, never as a template to copy. And fix the deliverables in writing before filming: the video count, raw footage yes or no, and the usage scope — Guide 3 below covers that properly.

The mistake that kills it

Writing the creator a word-for-word script. The entire value of UGC is that it does not sound written by the brand. Hand over the message, the must-shows and the never-says — then let the creator say it the way they talk to their own audience.

Guide 2 · Creative

What makes a product video convert on TikTok & Reels

Converting UGC looks like a review, not a commercial. Nobody honest can promise you a number — but the videos stores keep reordering share the same six mechanics:

  • Open on the product in use. The pour, the swipe, the before-state — not a greeting, not a logo. The viewer decides in that first moment whether this is for them.
  • One video, one idea. A single benefit, shown once and clearly. Five selling points is five videos — which is exactly why stores buy batches, not one-offs.
  • Proof over polish. Real hands, a real kitchen counter, natural light, the creator's actual voice. Studio gloss signals "ad" and gets skipped; a believable setting signals "someone like me tried this".
  • Say the offer out loud — and caption it. Price, bundle, or the reason to buy now, spoken by the creator and repeated as on-screen text for silent viewers. End on the next step: where to tap, what to look for.
  • Treat the first frame as a thumbnail. A readable overlay naming the problem — "POV: your foundation separates by noon" — gives the scroll a reason to stop.
  • Rotate angles, keep winners. Problem/solution, honest comparison, three reasons, the product inside a daily routine. Film several, run them, and reorder the ones your customers answer.

Guide 3 · Rights

Usage rights & whitelisting, explained for store owners

Receiving the video file is not the same as owning every use of it. This is where stores most often get caught out — so here is the whole subject, plainly:

Usage rights: what you are actually agreeing

Every creator video carries a usage scope: where it may appear (your product pages, your organic posts, your paid ad accounts), on which platforms, and for how long. Organic posting and paid advertising are different uses, and paid use is normally scoped by duration. Whatever you agree, get it in writing before production — a chat screenshot is a weak substitute once a campaign is live.

Whitelisting: ads through the creator's handle

Whitelisting means the ad runs through the creator's own account — the viewer sees the creator's name and face, with your budget behind it. On TikTok it runs through Spark Ads authorisation, which the creator generates from their account for a set period; on Meta, through partnership permissions granted to your ad account. It is arranged per creator, never automatic, and it belongs in the agreement with its duration.

Raw footage

If your team re-cuts content in-house, ask for the raw files in the original brief. Upfront, raws are one line in a quote; after delivery, they are a renegotiation.

Your pre-payment checklist

  • Number of videos and formats — vertical edits, any cut-downs
  • Edited files — and raw footage, yes or no
  • Usage scope: product pages / organic / paid — and for how long
  • Whitelisting included? For which creators, and what duration
  • Delivery schedule, tied to when your products reach the creators

Guide 4 · UAE market

Selling into the UAE from your online store: product content in two languages

The UAE is a two-language checkout. Your customer in Dubai or Abu Dhabi may discover the product in an Arabic video, check the details on an English page, and ask the final question in whichever language the chat opens in. Stores that treat one language as a translation of the other lose the sale at exactly one of those steps.

Native content in both languages, not a translation

Commission the Arabic and English versions as two originals: an Arabic-speaking creator films the Arabic cut in their own voice, and the English cut is written for English speakers — not subtitled from the Arabic script. Product pages mirror the same rule: full Arabic and full English descriptions, sizes and delivery information, each readable on its own.

Creators your UAE audience recognises

Where the product allows it, film with creators who live the market — the delivery routine, the malls and districts, the weekend rhythm — because UAE shoppers spot imported context instantly. Where you film remotely, brief the local details in writing: currency as printed on your store, delivery areas you actually serve, and the claims your licences allow.

Rights that cover both versions and both uses

Settle usage for the Arabic and English cuts together: product pages, organic posting and paid ads, with durations per use — the same discipline as any creator agreement, doubled across languages so neither version becomes the one you cannot run as an ad.

FAQ

What store owners ask before their first pack

Short answers, in writing — the same terms that end up in your agreement.

Who owns the videos — can we run them on our product pages and in our ads?

Usage is settled in writing before production starts. Your brief and quote name exactly where each video may run — product pages, your organic channels, your paid ad accounts — on which platforms, and for how long. The scope you buy is the scope in your agreement; nothing is left implied.

What is whitelisting — can ads run through the creator's own account?

Yes — whitelisting runs the ad through the creator's handle instead of your brand page. On TikTok it works through Spark Ads authorisation; on Meta, through partnership permissions the creator grants your ad account. It is arranged per creator, for an agreed period, and written into the agreement when your pack includes it.

How long does a UGC batch take?

The exact schedule is confirmed in a written plan before production starts. Timing depends on the number of videos and creators, creator availability, and how quickly your products reach the creators — so the plan is built around your shipping timeline rather than a generic promise.

Do we ship our products to the creators? Who manages it?

Yes — creators film with the real product in hand, so your store ships it. We coordinate the creator list, delivery details and follow-up, and confirm receipt before filming starts, so a batch never stalls on a missing parcel.

What do we receive at delivery — edited videos only, or raw footage too?

You receive finished, edited vertical videos, ready for product pages and ad accounts. Raw footage is included when your brief asks for it — say so upfront if your team re-cuts in-house, because adding raws to the original agreement is a line in a quote; asking after delivery is a renegotiation.

Are prices published?

No — no prices are published. Packs are structured (a defined number of videos, formats and usage scope) and priced in a written quote after a free consultation, based on the creators, videos and usage your store actually needs.
