Guide 1 · Briefing

How to brief a UGC creator for a product video

A good creator video starts with a one-page brief — not a script. The brief's job is to hand over everything the creator needs to be accurate, then leave them room to sound like themselves. Here is the structure we use:

What goes in the brief

  • The product and who it is for — one line each. "A leave-in conditioner for curly hair, sold to women who stopped straightening" beats three paragraphs of brand history.
  • The one problem it solves — pick a single angle per video. A video about everything converts on nothing.
  • Three must-show moments — e.g. the texture, the product in actual use, the result next to something familiar for scale. Creators film better when they know which shots are non-negotiable.
  • What must never be said — claims you cannot back up, medical or results promises your category restricts, competitor names. This line protects you, not just the creator.
  • Format basics — vertical (9:16), the product in use in the opening moment rather than a logo intro, and captions, because a large share of viewing is silent.

What to send with it

Ship the product early — filming cannot start before it arrives — and if you already have a post or ad that performed well, send it as a reference for tone, not as a template to copy. Then agree the deliverables in writing before filming: how many videos, whether raw footage is included, and the usage scope (Guide 3 below covers that properly).

The mistake to avoid

Writing the creator a word-for-word script. The entire value of UGC is that it does not sound written by the brand. Give the message, the must-shows and the never-says — and let the creator say it the way they talk to their own audience.

Guide 2 · Creative

What makes a product video convert on TikTok & Reels

Converting UGC looks like a review, not a commercial. Nobody can promise you a number — anyone who does is inventing it — but the videos that stores keep reordering share the same mechanics:

  • Open on the product in use. The first moment should show the thing working — the pour, the swipe, the before-state — not a greeting or a logo. A viewer decides in that opening whether this is for them.
  • One video, one idea. A single benefit, shown once, clearly. If the product has five selling points, that is five videos — which is exactly why stores buy batches, not one-offs.
  • Proof over polish. Real hands, a real kitchen counter, natural light, the creator's actual voice. Studio gloss signals "ad" and gets skipped; a believable setting signals "someone like me tried this".
  • Say the offer out loud — and put it in text. Price, bundle, or the reason to buy now, spoken by the creator and repeated as an on-screen caption for silent viewers. End with the next step: where to tap, what to look for.
  • Treat the first frame as a thumbnail. A readable text overlay naming the problem ("POV: your foundation separates by noon") gives the scroll a reason to stop.
  • Rotate angles, keep winners. Problem/solution, honest comparison, "three reasons", the product inside a daily routine — film several angles, run them, and reorder the ones your customers respond to.

Guide 3 · Rights

Usage rights & whitelisting, explained for store owners

Receiving the video file is not the same as owning every use of it. This is the part of UGC that stores most often get wrong — so here it is plainly:

Usage rights: what you are actually agreeing

A creator's video has a usage scope: where it may appear (your product pages, your brand's organic social posts, your paid ad accounts), on which platforms, and for how long. Organic posting and paid advertising are different uses, and paid use is normally scoped by duration. Whatever you agree, get it in writing before production — a screenshot of a chat is a weak substitute when a campaign is live.

Whitelisting: ads through the creator's handle

Whitelisting means your ad runs through the creator's own account instead of your brand page — the viewer sees the creator's name and face, with your budget behind it. On TikTok this is done through Spark Ads authorisation (the creator generates an authorisation from their account, for a set period); on Meta, through partnership/branded-content permissions the creator grants your ad account. It has to be arranged per creator — it is never automatic, and it should be written into the agreement with its duration.

Raw footage

If your team re-cuts content in-house, ask for the raw files in the original brief. Including raws upfront is a line in a quote; asking after delivery is a renegotiation.

Your pre-payment checklist

  • Number of videos and formats (vertical edits, any cut-downs)
  • Edited files — and raw footage, yes or no
  • Usage scope: product pages / organic / paid ads — and for how long
  • Whitelisting included? For which creators, and what duration
  • Delivery schedule, tied to when your products reach the creators

06 — FAQ

What store owners ask before their first pack

Who owns the videos — can we use them on our product pages and in our ads?

Usage is agreed in writing before production starts. The brief and quote spell out where the videos may be used — your product pages, your organic channels, your paid ad accounts — on which platforms, and for how long. The usage scope you buy is the usage scope written in your agreement.

What is whitelisting, and can we run ads through the creator's own account?

Whitelisting means the ad runs through the creator's own handle instead of your brand's page — on TikTok via Spark Ads authorisation, on Meta via partnership permissions the creator grants your ad account, for an agreed period. When it is part of your pack, it is arranged per creator and written into the agreement. Guide 3 above explains it in full.

How long does a UGC batch take?

The exact schedule is confirmed in a written plan before production starts. It depends on the number of videos and creators, creator availability, and how fast your products reach the creators — so the plan is built around your shipping timeline, not a generic promise.

Do we ship our products to the creators? Who handles that?

Yes — creators need the real product in hand to film honest content. Your store ships the products; we coordinate the creator list, delivery details and follow-up, and confirm receipt before filming starts, so no batch stalls on a missing parcel.

What do we receive at delivery — edited videos only, or the raw footage too?

You receive the finished, edited vertical videos, ready for product pages and ad accounts. Raw footage can be included when it is agreed in the brief — say so upfront if your team re-cuts content in-house; it is far easier to include raws in the original agreement than to renegotiate them later.

Are prices published?

No prices are published. Packs are structured — a defined number of videos, formats and usage scope — and priced in a written quote after a free consultation or brief, based on the number of creators, videos and usage you need.
